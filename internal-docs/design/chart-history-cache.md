# Durable display history

`market.history` now selects an applicable saved series before requiring an IBKR connection. The daemon owns acquisition, persistence and coverage; RPC carries the selected records and clocks. CLI/MCP/Desk remain readers. This cache grants no account, valuation, decision or broker-write authority.

Previously the general chart route kept a one-minute process cache, checked broker availability first and lost records on restart. The new path saves validated close/volume points as one current `market_chart_history.current.v1` document per normalized request contract and interval in corestore. Resolved contract, pricing basis, regular-hours convention and timestamp kind are checked before merging. Failed/cancelled or malformed responses cannot overwrite the previous record. Futures require a conid and exact expiry. Historical charts are public-market data, not account snapshots.

Retention is five years plus one boundary day for daily data and twenty observed venue dates for intraday data, applied on successful acquisition. Frozen inactive documents remain available until refreshed or evicted. Caps are 8,000 points/2 MiB per document, 750 documents and 512 MiB total JSON. Oldest updated chart documents are evicted; other state kinds and evidence remain untouched. SQLite/WAL/backups add physical overhead. Synthetic full-OHLCV measurements suggest 35–91 MiB per 100 instruments; allow twice this for planning. This is not measured production consumption.

Daily view ranges share a document. Initial/wider requests acquire the requested range; incremental reads request the tail with three calendar days of overlap. Refresh cadences are five minutes, thirty minutes and one hour. A seven-day daily reconciliation or changed older daily overlap requests the retained depth, at most five years. A full read replaces the recorded sessions of the span it requested and refuses a response that lacks one of them older than two days, so a thin response cannot erase history. IBKR serves a dated futures contract's daily bars only about a year back, a window that rolls forward with the clock, so for futures the replaced span starts at the first bar served: recorded sessions older than that are beyond the served depth and are retained unreconciled, and a dated contract's prices are never adjusted, so nothing is spliced. Until 25 September 2026 the requested span applied to futures too: an equity-index contract made front at its quarterly roll, a year after listing, recorded sessions at that window's edge, and from its first weekly reconciliation every refresh was refused and its 1Y series stopped. `full_read_at` records the last read that replaced everything a series can re-read: for daily bars, a full read that reaches the start still retained after pruning; for intraday bars, any full read of its request window, because twenty retained venue dates outlast every intraday window and no read returns to them. Intraday series therefore also run one full read of their window per week and tail reads otherwise. Until 25 September 2026 intraday reconciliation had to reach the retained range, which no rolling 1D or 5D read does, so from a week after a series' first read every refresh ran as a full read and was served as refresh due. Existing connector pacing and a two-request limit remain binding. No TWS reconnect or broker write is introduced.

A server-owned worker maintains up to 64 interval/instrument interests, using thirty-second checks, a four-minute background-lane request window and retry backoff capped at fifteen minutes. User chart reads register 24-hour interest; market snapshot observations register current instruments' 1D/1Y interests even without a browser. There is no per-tick database write. Startup starts the worker after server context ownership is established; shutdown cancels and joins it before corestore closes. Foreground requests retain their existing request lifetime.

Known US sessions govern 1D selection and completed-session refresh. A cold 1D acquisition requests at most six calendar days of five-minute bars, covering supported weekend and holiday closures before SMART resolves to a venue. Selection then uses the resolved contract; cache freshness and incremental reads use that same retained identity without changing the request key. US stocks use 04:00 for a new premarket view and 20:15 for extended-hours read completion; daily records use a fifteen-minute post-close allowance. A 1D read whose newest bar precedes the selected day is an answer, not a failure: the day ending at that bar is recorded, the read's fetch clock advances, and selection labels it the previous window until the day's first bar arrives. That covers a closed venue without an embedded calendar, a US day before its first bar prints, and IBKR's delayed feed, which trails the market by up to twenty minutes, so an unentitled index has no bar of the new session for that long after the open. Until 25 September 2026 a US venue's such read was refused: the record kept its premarket fetch clock and was served refresh due while the worker backed it off past the first delayed bar. Unrecognized venues remain time-based and are not claimed calendar-complete. Known missing US daily sessions are reported; absent intraday trades, listing dates and unsupported calendars are not inferred. A successful daily read is not certified final settlement.

Selection metadata distinguishes `selected`, `stored_at`, `fetched_at`, `covered_through`, `coverage`, `missing_sessions`, `refresh_due`, `refresh_failed` and `previous_window`. `refresh_due` means the record is behind and the worker has had its whole cycle (thirty-second tick plus four-minute window) to catch it up: it lacks a prefix the view needs, or its bars are older than their cadence plus that cycle while the venue may have traded. A record inside its normal cycle, or awaiting only its weekly reconciliation, is not refresh due; the daemon still reads the broker at the plain cadence and at reconciliation. Reading the cache changes no evidence clock. `observed` coverage is not a universal completeness guarantee. Cached records are returned even when a refresh fails; an uncached request remains explicitly unavailable.

Regression tests use synthetic instruments and temporary databases. They cover broker-free reload, failed refresh, cancellation, cross-contract rejection, incremental-prefix retention, thin-response preservation, missing daily sessions, memory-only durability disclosure, retention, weekend/premarket behavior, corrected overlap, worker joining and eviction isolation. No live disconnect or order operation is used. The consumer documentation in Desk records browser and installation evidence separately.

Validation on the cache candidate: `make modernize` completed; its iterator rewrite was reviewed. `make test` passed on 12 September 2026, including default/trading daemon race tests, hermetic lifecycle integration and repository gates. In a disposable staged-tree copy, removing `s.marketData.loopWG.Wait()` made `TestMarketHistoryWorkerJoinsShutdown` fail with “shutdown passed an in-flight history publication”; the unmodified suite passes. This proves the join guard is exercised, not live broker quality or historical completeness.
